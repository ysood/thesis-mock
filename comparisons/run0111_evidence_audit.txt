alternative routes plus reference defects. The report understates explicit defects in the described
arrangements and accepts an asserted waiver under a permissive prompt rule without supporting
documents. Stronger evidence handling should distinguish assertions, actual documents, failed
described routes, unestablished alternatives, and reference-check failures.

A06. The public-interest recognition finding is more confident than its support

Stronger explanation: Potential public-interest route identified, but the legal link to this
category of international transfer and operation-specific necessity remain unestablished. Treat
recognition as a reasoned legal question requiring support, not a cleared requirement merely because
research can serve the public interest.

A07. GDPR applicability is supported, but recipient obligations need separate scope
Observed: The Irish sender and retained linking key support treating its disclosure as GDPR
processing of personal data. That is the sole met main requirement, a finding of applicability
rather than compliance. The recipient's independent GDPR territorial scope is expressly left open,
while later findings demand an Article 6 basis for both controllers.

Assessment: The analysis should identify whether a recipient duty arises directly under Article 3,
through an applicable transfer arrangement, or only as an assumption. Receiving EU data does not by
itself resolve direct territorial scope. Controller labels also do not establish joint control; an
Article 26 arrangement or Article 28 contract is only relevant if the corresponding relationship is
established.

Stronger explanation: The Irish controller's processing and export are within the GDPR review.
Assess its basis now. State the assumed recipient relationship and territorial scope before making a
separate recipient Article 6 basis or role-specific contract a decisive missing predicate.
